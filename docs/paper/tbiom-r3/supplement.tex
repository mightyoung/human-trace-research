\section{Environment}
Python 3.11; \texttt{numpy}, \texttt{scipy}, \texttt{scikit-learn}, \texttt{opencv-python}, \texttt{onnxruntime-gpu}. \emph{Detector:} YOLO8, person class, official pretrained weights, used without site fine-tuning in all three domains. The domains differ in sampling stride and tracker settings, not in detector; RF-DETR was benchmarked as a candidate replacement on a separate detection set and is not behind any number in the article. \emph{Tracker:} ByteTrack with \texttt{track\_high\_thresh} 0.10, \texttt{track\_low\_thresh} 0.05, \texttt{new\_track\_thresh} 0.12, \texttt{match\_thresh} 0.85 and \texttt{fuse\_score} enabled; \texttt{track\_buffer} is 60 at a sampling stride of 5 for the build (gallery) portions and 100 at a stride of 3 for the query portions, the buffer being counted in processed frames, so both cover about 12~s of real time. \emph{Appearance:} OSNet-x1.0 trained on MSMT17, used as-is. \emph{Face:} SCRFD-2.5G detection, five-point alignment, ArcFace 512-d embeddings, using \texttt{w600k\_r50} for the D1 pixel-budget study and \texttt{w600k\_mbf} for the D2 selector pool. \emph{Second image encoder, E2:} EmbeddingGemma-2, 768-d image embeddings.

\section{Gait as a candidate fourth probe}
Both populations are D1. Of the 1,473 D1 tracks that are evaluable here --- 305: 182, 316: 216, 318: 381, 320: 145, 323: 469, 343: 80 --- 204 (13.8\%) contain a walking segment of at least two seconds whose median box height exceeds 100~px. Of the 538 D1 observations that carry a gallery identity label, 74 do. The gap between the two denominators is the point: most tracks have no identity label, and most labelled observations have no usable walking segment. On the 63 tracks that also pass a silhouette quality gate (34 identities, 1,953 pairs, 44 evaluable rank-1 queries), a keypoint descriptor reaches AUC 0.613 (95\% CI 0.496--0.720), a silhouette-dynamics descriptor 0.632 (0.534--0.725) and a gait energy image 0.656 (0.547--0.766), against 0.865 (0.796--0.926) for appearance on the same tracks, and 0\% to 11.4\% rank-1 against 45.5\%. Fusing gait into the appearance score degrades ranking monotonically (AUC 0.778 at weight 0.2, 0.684 at 0.5). The residual gait signal is stronger in cross-camera pairs (0.670) than same-camera pairs (0.575), which suggests body build more than gait phase. Gait needs a walking segment, sufficient body height and a side view; a floor of workstations rarely supplies them. We therefore propose three probes.

\section{External check of P2 on WILDTRACK}\label{sec:supp-wildtrack}

\emph{Why this check.} Section V-B claims only that a region observed by both cameras is where L1 may be attempted; an earlier draft claimed that such a region makes L1 available. To test the stronger claim we need a site with a global identity label and calibrated views, which our floor does not supply. WILDTRACK (T.~Chavdarova \emph{et al.}, CVPR 2018; cited as~[14] in the main text) provides seven synchronised cameras over one plaza with global person IDs.

\emph{Protocol.} 400 annotated frames; 20 frames per view sampled evenly; 1{,}855 crops with 8\% padding; OSNet-x1.0 (MSMT17), 512-d, $\ell_2$-normalised. Co-visibility of a pair is $\sum_t \mathbf{1}[\text{visible in both}] / \sum_t \mathbf{1}[\text{visible in either}]$ over all 400 frames. Positive pairs are the same personID in two views at different times; negatives are random different-personID pairs of equal count. AUC is Mann--Whitney with identity-level bootstrap (1{,}000 draws).

\emph{Co-visibility does not order pairs by AUC.} Across 21 pairs, co-visibility spans 0.00--0.89 and cross-time AUC spans 0.350--0.901 (median 0.755). The two correlate at $r=0.179$ ($\rho=0.161$), and at $r=-0.05$ once pairs with fewer than 30 shared identities are dropped. Banding by co-visibility leaves the medians flat: 0.739, 0.817 and 0.755 for co-visibility $\ge0.5$, 0.2--0.5 and $<0.2$.

\emph{An identity-composition confound, and its control.} Raw pair AUC falls as the shared identity set grows (228 identities: 0.711; 43 identities: 0.901), because the people two distant cameras both see are the salient ones. Resampling 15 identities per pair 200 times removes the trend ($\rho=-0.153$) and leaves the null intact. Any per-pair AUC comparison at a multi-camera site should control this.

\emph{Region-level A/B.} Box feet are back-projected to the ground plane through the calibrations; a pair of crops counts as in-region when both feet fall in grid cells visible to both cameras (60~cm grid). Of 21 pairs, 17 admit both an in-region and an out-region estimate:

Foot-point back-projection assumes upright persons on a plane; the region test also inherits the recovered camera-index mapping, and no alternative assignment was evaluated. The 21 pairs share seven cameras and one person pool, so they are not 21 independent site tests.

\section{Sensitivity of the L2+ selector to a box-height floor and to the decision rule}\label{sec:supp-sens}

The 179 queries behind the L2+ row of the main text are not filtered by person-box height, and neither are the morning library tracks. Applying a 60~px floor to both --- the operating point of the selector we deploy --- leaves 157 queries and changes what the box-height kernel contributes.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Selector recall at $\tau=0.80$ on D2 under a $2\times2$ box-height floor.}
\label{tab:sens}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Library & Query & $n$ & Plain mean & Kernel \\
\midrule
unfiltered & unfiltered & 179 & 101 & 125 \\
unfiltered & $\ge 60$~px & 157 & 89 & 109 \\
$\ge 60$~px & unfiltered & 179 & 101 & 121 \\
$\ge 60$~px & $\ge 60$~px & 157 & 100 & 108 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Either floor alone leaves the kernel a gain of 20 queries; both together leave it 8. The third row shows why: filtering the library already lifts the plain mean on the surviving queries from 89 to 100, which is most of what the kernel was doing. We therefore report the L2+ gain as conditional on an unfiltered library, not as an increment that survives the quality gate we deploy.

A second sensitivity concerns the decision rule rather than the representation. Replacing the absolute test $s_1\ge\tau$ by a relative one, $s_1-s_2\ge m$, moves recall at a fixed number of false accepts. With library and queries unfiltered, one false accept admits 116 queries against 56 for the absolute rule, and four admit 118 against 112; the kernel reaches 130 at four. The two remedies do not combine: applying both gives 115 at four false accepts, consistent with both acting on the same score-scale defect. Because the relative rule ignores absolute similarity, it applies only to the closed-set setting of this experiment and would need an absolute floor in open-set deployment.

\section{Operating cost}
\emph{Adjudication.} 27 items in 20--30 minutes by one adjudicator on a phone, offline: about 45--67~s per item, or 30--40 items per operator-hour. The adjudication surface is one self-contained HTML file with embedded images (2.05~MB for 27 items); verdicts export as JSON. Generating it takes about 10 minutes on first run.

\emph{Probes.} P1 and P3 run on existing detections in under an hour each. P2 is a site task of about half a day for six cameras. Its external check (Section~\ref{sec:supp-wildtrack}) costs about four minutes of GPU time once 1{,}855 crops exist, plus roughly twenty minutes of calibration handling.

\emph{What was not measured.} Queue length beyond a 35-minute capture, and adjudication cost as a function of queue length.
